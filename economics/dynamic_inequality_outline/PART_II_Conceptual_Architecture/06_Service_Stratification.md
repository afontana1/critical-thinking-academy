# Chapter 6 — Service Stratification

**Guiding question:** How do unequal services become a mechanism of cumulative inequality?

## Chapter deliverable

A finished chapter should make one clear argument, define every concept used later, distinguish established mechanisms from original synthesis, and end by motivating the next chapter.

## Proposed structure

### Why services matter

- Services mediate conversion of resources into capabilities and outcomes.

### Domains

- Education, healthcare, finance, housing, transport, legal services, digital infrastructure, and administration.

### Composite versus disaggregated

- State when a composite service stock is acceptable and when disaggregation is required.

### Accumulation and depreciation

- Public/private investment, maintenance, congestion, learning, and deterioration.

### Spatial feedback

- Sorting, tax base, infrastructure finance, access costs, and place-based persistence.

### Equation preview

$$
S_g(t+1)=S_g(t)+I^S_g(t)-\delta_S S_g(t)
$$

### Measurement

- Distinguish access, utilization, affordability, and quality.

## Evidence to collect

- Primary theoretical source for every major mechanism.
- Best empirical estimate for each causal relationship or parameter.
- Scope conditions and conflicting findings.

## Planned figures and tables

- One figure only when it clarifies structure, behavior, or measurement.
- One table mapping claims to evidence, variables, equations, or data when useful.

## Writing prompts

- What must the reader believe by the end of this chapter?
- Which claims are literature-backed, and which are hypotheses?
- Which terms require operational definitions?
- Which causal arrows require direct evidence?
- What assumptions or boundary conditions must be explicit?
- How does the final paragraph motivate the next chapter?
