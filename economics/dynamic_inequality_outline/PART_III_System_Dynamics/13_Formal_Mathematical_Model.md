# Chapter 13 — Formal Mathematical Specification

**Guiding question:** What equations operationalize the theory?

## Chapter deliverable

A finished chapter should make one clear argument, define every concept used later, distinguish established mechanisms from original synthesis, and end by motivating the next chapter.

## Proposed structure

### Notation

- Indices, time, units, weights, normalization, and bounds.

### Assets and wealth

$$
A_g(t+1)=A_g(t)+I^A_g(t)+R^A_g(t)-L^A_g(t)
$$
$$
W_g(t)=A_g(t)-D_g(t)
$$

### Debt

$$
D_g(t+1)=D_g(t)+B_g(t)+r^D_g(t)D_g(t)-Rep_g(t)-WriteOff_g(t)
$$

### Income

$$
Y_g(t)=Y^L_g(t)+Y^K_g(t)+Tr_g(t)-Tax_g(t)
$$

### Human capital

$$
H_g(t+1)=H_g(t)+I^H_g(t)-\delta_H H_g(t)
$$

### Services

$$
S_g(t+1)=S_g(t)+I^S_g(t)-\delta_S S_g(t)
$$

### Credit

- Specify borrowing limit, interest rate, and default functions.

### Networks and influence

- Specify formation, decay, and policy mapping.

### Legitimacy and disruption

- Specify fairness, erosion, escalation, and recovery.

### Intergenerational transfer

- Specify parent-child stocks and transition probabilities.

### Inequality measures

- Gini, top shares, log variance, between-group gaps, mobility, and multidimensional opportunity inequality.

### Nonlinearities

- Borrowing constraints, default thresholds, minimum services, saturation, and policy thresholds.

### Reduced model

- Provide a compact model for testing and identification.

### Parameter registry

- Definition, unit, sign, range, source, and calibration method.

## Evidence to collect

- Primary theoretical source for every major mechanism.
- Best empirical estimate for each causal relationship or parameter.
- Scope conditions and conflicting findings.

## Planned figures and tables

- One figure only when it clarifies structure, behavior, or measurement.
- One table mapping claims to evidence, variables, equations, or data when useful.

## Writing prompts

- What must the reader believe by the end of this chapter?
- Which claims are literature-backed, and which are hypotheses?
- Which terms require operational definitions?
- Which causal arrows require direct evidence?
- What assumptions or boundary conditions must be explicit?
- How does the final paragraph motivate the next chapter?

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Opening states the chapter problem.
- [ ] Concepts and notation are consistent.
- [ ] Major causal claims are sourced or labeled as hypotheses.
- [ ] Equations are interpretable and dimensionally plausible.
- [ ] Figures and tables have analytical purposes.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit.
- [ ] Closing advances the monograph narrative.
