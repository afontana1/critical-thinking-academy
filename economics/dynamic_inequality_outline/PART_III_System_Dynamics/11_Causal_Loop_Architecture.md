# Chapter 11 — Causal-Loop Architecture

**Guiding question:** Which feedback loops generate persistence, divergence, convergence, or instability?

## Chapter deliverable

A finished chapter should make one clear argument, define every concept used later, distinguish established mechanisms from original synthesis, and end by motivating the next chapter.

## Proposed structure

### Diagram conventions

- Signs, delays, endogenous/exogenous variables, and loop naming.

### R1 Asset accumulation

- Assets → capital income → saving → assets.

### R2 Wealth-credit-investment

- Collateral → borrowing terms → investment → assets → collateral.

### R3 Services-human capital-income

- Resources → services → human capital → labor income → resources.

### R4 Debt and precarity

- Low wealth → costly debt → default/asset loss → lower wealth.

### R5 Political allocation

- Wealth → influence → policy → taxes/returns/services → wealth.

### R6 Intergenerational persistence

- Parent resources → child opportunity → child outcomes → next-generation resources.

### R7 Legitimacy and instability

- Inequality → exclusion → legitimacy loss → disruption → service decline → inequality.

### Balancing loops

- Redistribution, universal services, social insurance, progressive taxation, debt relief, and anti-capture institutions.

### Loop interaction

- Identify delays, thresholds, tipping points, and changing loop dominance.

### Boundary critique

- List omitted feedbacks and justify exclusions.

## Evidence to collect

- Primary theoretical source for every major mechanism.
- Best empirical estimate for each causal relationship or parameter.
- Scope conditions and conflicting findings.

## Planned figures and tables

- One figure only when it clarifies structure, behavior, or measurement.
- One table mapping claims to evidence, variables, equations, or data when useful.

## Writing prompts

- What must the reader believe by the end of this chapter?
- Which claims are literature-backed, and which are hypotheses?
- Which terms require operational definitions?
- Which causal arrows require direct evidence?
- What assumptions or boundary conditions must be explicit?
- How does the final paragraph motivate the next chapter?

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Opening states the chapter problem.
- [ ] Concepts and notation are consistent.
- [ ] Major causal claims are sourced or labeled as hypotheses.
- [ ] Equations are interpretable and dimensionally plausible.
- [ ] Figures and tables have analytical purposes.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit.
- [ ] Closing advances the monograph narrative.
