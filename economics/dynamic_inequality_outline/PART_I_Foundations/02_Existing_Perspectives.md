# Chapter 2 — Existing Perspectives and the Integration Gap

**Guiding question:** What do existing theories explain well, and what remains fragmented?

## Chapter deliverable

A finished chapter should make one clear argument, define every concept used later, distinguish established mechanisms from original synthesis, and end by motivating the next chapter.

## Proposed structure

### Human capital

- Education, health, skills, experience, and labor-market returns.
- Limitation: access to investment is often treated as exogenous.

### Credit constraints

- Collateral, borrowing access, borrowing cost, liquidity, default, and underinvestment.

### Wealth accumulation

- Saving, capital income, appreciation, inheritance, and heterogeneous returns.

### Mobility and intergenerational transmission

- Parent resources, neighborhoods, education, networks, inheritance, and transition probabilities.

### Spatial and service inequality

- Schools, healthcare, finance, housing, transportation, legal services, and public goods.

### Political economy

- Wealth, influence, taxation, regulation, and public investment.

### Legitimacy and instability

- Perceived fairness, trust, compliance, conflict, state capacity, and service deterioration.

### Systems and cumulative causation

- Feedback, delays, nonlinearity, thresholds, path dependence, and emergence.

### Integration gap

- Show which mechanisms are usually studied separately and why integration adds value.

## Evidence to collect

- Primary theoretical source for every major mechanism.
- Best empirical estimate for each causal relationship or parameter.
- Scope conditions and conflicting findings.

## Planned figures and tables

- One figure only when it clarifies structure, behavior, or measurement.
- One table mapping claims to evidence, variables, equations, or data when useful.

## Writing prompts

- What must the reader believe by the end of this chapter?
- Which claims are literature-backed, and which are hypotheses?
- Which terms require operational definitions?
- Which causal arrows require direct evidence?
- What assumptions or boundary conditions must be explicit?
- How does the final paragraph motivate the next chapter?

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Opening states the chapter problem.
- [ ] Concepts and notation are consistent.
- [ ] Major causal claims are sourced or labeled as hypotheses.
- [ ] Equations are interpretable and dimensionally plausible.
- [ ] Figures and tables have analytical purposes.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit.
- [ ] Closing advances the monograph narrative.
