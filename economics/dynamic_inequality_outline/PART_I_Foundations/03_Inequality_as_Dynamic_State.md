# Chapter 3 — Inequality as a Dynamic State

**Guiding question:** What does it mean to treat inequality as an endogenous state variable?

## Chapter deliverable

A finished chapter should make one clear argument, define every concept used later, distinguish established mechanisms from original synthesis, and end by motivating the next chapter.

## Proposed structure

### From distribution to state

- Distinguish a cross-sectional distribution from a condition that affects later dynamics.

### Dynamic asymmetry

- Define asymmetry in assets, debt, human capital, services, networks, political access, and risk-bearing capacity.

### Opportunity sets

- Define opportunity as the set of feasible transitions rather than a vague resource.

### Endogeneity and feedback

- Show how inequality enters the causal graph as both output and input.

### Time and generations

- Separate short-run, life-course, and intergenerational delays.

### Formal preview

- Use the group-level state vector below and explain each component.
$$
\mathbf{x}_g(t)=\left[A_g,D_g,H_g,S_g,N_g,P_g,L_g\right]
$$

### Scope conditions

- List conditions under which feedbacks weaken, reverse, or require added mechanisms.

## Evidence to collect

- Primary theoretical source for every major mechanism.
- Best empirical estimate for each causal relationship or parameter.
- Scope conditions and conflicting findings.

## Planned figures and tables

- One figure only when it clarifies structure, behavior, or measurement.
- One table mapping claims to evidence, variables, equations, or data when useful.

## Writing prompts

- What must the reader believe by the end of this chapter?
- Which claims are literature-backed, and which are hypotheses?
- Which terms require operational definitions?
- Which causal arrows require direct evidence?
- What assumptions or boundary conditions must be explicit?
- How does the final paragraph motivate the next chapter?
