# Chapter 1 — Introduction

**Guiding question:** Why does persistent inequality require a dynamic explanation?

## Chapter deliverable

A finished chapter should make one clear argument, define every concept used later, distinguish established mechanisms from original synthesis, and end by motivating the next chapter.

## Proposed structure

### The empirical puzzle

- Document persistent gaps in wealth, income, mobility, health, education, and institutional access.
- Distinguish inequality, poverty, stratification, and immobility.

### Limits of outcome-only explanations

- Show how inequality is commonly treated as a dependent variable.
- Explain why current inequality also changes future opportunities and constraints.

### Core thesis

- Define inequality as a dynamic asymmetry in accumulated resources, capabilities, risks, and institutional access.
- State that inequality is both an outcome and an endogenous input.

### Research questions

- When does inequality reproduce itself?
- Which feedback loops dominate?
- When do balancing mechanisms work?
- How can the model be verified and calibrated?

### Contribution

- Integrate established mechanisms into a coupled stock–flow system.
- Bridge theory, simulation, calibration, and policy experiments.

### Scope and roadmap

- Choose household/group/region/generation as the first unit.
- State exclusions and preview the monograph.

## Evidence to collect

- Primary theoretical source for every major mechanism.
- Best empirical estimate for each causal relationship or parameter.
- Scope conditions and conflicting findings.

## Planned figures and tables

- One figure only when it clarifies structure, behavior, or measurement.
- One table mapping claims to evidence, variables, equations, or data when useful.

## Writing prompts

- What must the reader believe by the end of this chapter?
- Which claims are literature-backed, and which are hypotheses?
- Which terms require operational definitions?
- Which causal arrows require direct evidence?
- What assumptions or boundary conditions must be explicit?
- How does the final paragraph motivate the next chapter?

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Opening states the chapter problem.
- [ ] Concepts and notation are consistent.
- [ ] Major causal claims are sourced or labeled as hypotheses.
- [ ] Equations are interpretable and dimensionally plausible.
- [ ] Figures and tables have analytical purposes.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit.
- [ ] Closing advances the monograph narrative.
