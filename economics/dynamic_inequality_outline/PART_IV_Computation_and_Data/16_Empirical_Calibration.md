# Chapter 16 — Empirical Calibration

**Guiding question:** How will parameters and initial conditions be grounded in real data?

## Chapter deliverable

A finished chapter should make one clear argument, define every concept used later, distinguish established mechanisms from original synthesis, and end by motivating the next chapter.

## Proposed structure

### Calibration philosophy

- Direct measurement, econometric estimation, moment matching, Bayesian calibration, literature priors, and expert elicitation.

### Calibration unit

- Country, region, race/ethnicity, quantile, cohort, or household type.

### Candidate datasets

- SCF, PSID, Opportunity Insights, Census/ACS, IRS, FRED, OECD, WID, and sector-specific data.

### Initial conditions

- Assets, debt, human capital, services, networks, political variables, legitimacy, and disruption.

### Flow calibration

- Saving, investment, borrowing, repayment, education/health investment, service investment, taxes, and transfers.

### Functional forms

- Labor income, returns, credit access, default, opportunity, and mobility transitions.

### Harmonization

- Units, prices, weights, missing data, top coding, and cross-dataset linkage.

### Identification

- Equifinality, parameter correlation, profile likelihood, and synthetic recovery.

### Targets

- Wealth, debt, income, mobility, service gaps, default, and fiscal incidence.

### Reproducibility

- Raw data, scripts, parameter files, versioning, and metadata.

## Evidence to collect

- Primary theoretical source for every major mechanism.
- Best empirical estimate for each causal relationship or parameter.
- Scope conditions and conflicting findings.

## Planned figures and tables

- One figure only when it clarifies structure, behavior, or measurement.
- One table mapping claims to evidence, variables, equations, or data when useful.

## Writing prompts

- What must the reader believe by the end of this chapter?
- Which claims are literature-backed, and which are hypotheses?
- Which terms require operational definitions?
- Which causal arrows require direct evidence?
- What assumptions or boundary conditions must be explicit?
- How does the final paragraph motivate the next chapter?

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Opening states the chapter problem.
- [ ] Concepts and notation are consistent.
- [ ] Major causal claims are sourced or labeled as hypotheses.
- [ ] Equations are interpretable and dimensionally plausible.
- [ ] Figures and tables have analytical purposes.
- [ ] Limitations are explicit.
- [ ] Closing advances the monograph narrative.
