# Book II Outline
# Toward an Existential Capability Humanism
## Freedom, Flourishing, Complexity, and Humility

**Target length:** 175–275 pages  
**Purpose:** The rigorous theoretical defense of the framework introduced in Book I.  
**Central question:** *Can Existential Capability Humanism be defended as a coherent normative social philosophy?*

---

# Core Thesis

A defensible social philosophy must begin from the nature of human beings as embedded, relational, meaning-creating, sentient, and vulnerable agents. Freedom is not mere non-interference, but the capability-enabled capacity for becoming. Flourishing requires social, ecological, political, and material conditions that protect persons from domination and objectification. Because social reality is complex, relational, and historically contingent, institutions must be evaluated through methodological pluralism, epistemic humility, and adaptive design rather than ideological certainty.

# Conclusion

## 38. Existential Capability Humanism
Synthesize the framework:
1. Human beings are unfinished projects.
2. Freedom is capability-enabled becoming.
3. Flourishing requires social, material, ecological, and psychological conditions.
4. Objectification reduces subjects into objects.
5. Domination constrains open futures.
6. Reality exceeds all models.
7. Epistemic humility is a political virtue.
8. Institutions are coordination technologies.
9. Markets are embedded and contingent.
10. Democracy must resist oligarchy and ideological capture.
11. Ecological systems are the precondition for all flourishing.

Final formulation:

> Existential Capability Humanism is a normative social philosophy that evaluates institutions according to whether they expand the real capabilities of persons and other sentient beings to flourish as free, non-objectified, non-dominated participants in open futures, while remaining humble before the complexity, ambiguity, and relational character of reality.
